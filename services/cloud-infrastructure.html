Cloud & Infrastructure

Cloud Architecture and DevOps

We set up the cloud environments your product runs on, so they are secure, easy to release to and ready for more users. Then we stay on to keep an eye on them.

In short

Cloud infrastructure is the set of servers, networks, databases and automated pipelines that a software product runs on. At Fat Elephant we design the architecture, automate how code is tested and released, set up monitoring and security, and keep supporting the system after launch.

What We Do

What we do: Cloud & Infrastructure

Six areas of cloud work, used together or on their own. Most projects start with the architecture and add the rest as the product grows.

Cloud architecture

We design the layout of your environment: where things run, how they talk to each other and how they cope with growth or failure. A sound design at the start saves a great deal of cost and trouble later.

CI/CD pipelines

Automated steps that test your code and release it, so shipping a change is routine and not an event. Small, frequent releases are easier to check and easier to undo.

Infrastructure automation

We describe your environment in code, so it can be created, copied and repaired in a repeatable way. That means fewer manual steps, fewer surprises and a record of what changed.

Monitoring and observability

Dashboards and alerts that show how the system is behaving, so problems are spotted by us and not by your users. When something does go wrong, the information needed to find the cause is already there.

Security

Security is built into the design: careful control over who can reach what, protected data and keeping secrets out of code. We also plan for updates, so the system does not quietly fall out of date.

Ongoing support

Someone who knows your system looks after it after launch: watching it, applying updates and helping when something needs attention. You are not left alone with a system you did not build.

The Process

How it works

Three phases, one team. The people who design the environment work alongside the people who build the product that runs on it.

  1. 01

    Review and design

    We start with what your product needs: how many people will use it, where the data must live and what has to stay available. Then we design an environment that fits, no bigger than it has to be.

  2. 02

    Build and automate

    We set the environment up as code, connect the release pipeline and add monitoring and security from the first day, not as an afterthought.

  3. 03

    Run and improve

    We watch how it behaves, tune it as your usage changes and keep it up to date. If you prefer, we hand over clear documentation so your own team can take it on.

Honest Fit

Who it's for, and who it isn't

A good fit if you are

  • Teams launching a product who want a solid foundation from the start
  • Teams whose releases are manual, slow or stressful
  • Companies whose product is growing and starting to strain
  • Teams who want someone reliable watching over the system

Probably not for

  • Very small projects where simple managed hosting is enough. We'll say so, rather than over-build
  • Anyone who wants security as a box to tick. It only works when it is part of how you build and release
  • Teams who want us to choose tools before we understand the product
FAQ

Questions we get asked

What is CI/CD?

CI/CD stands for continuous integration and continuous delivery. In plain terms, it is an automated line that tests every change to your code and releases it safely, so shipping updates becomes routine.

What is infrastructure as code?

It means your servers, networks and settings are written down as code instead of set up by hand. The environment can then be rebuilt, copied or fixed the same way every time, and every change leaves a record.

Which cloud provider will you use?

We choose with you, based on what the product needs, where your data must live and what your team already knows. We avoid locking you in where we can.

How do you keep the system secure?

We limit who and what can reach each part of the system, protect data, keep secrets out of code, plan regular updates and monitor for anything unusual. Security is part of the design, not an extra layer on top.

What is observability?

Observability is being able to see what is happening inside your system: its speed, errors and load. It lets us find and fix problems quickly, often before users notice.

How long does it take, and what does it cost?

It depends on the size of the product and how much has to be set up and automated. Tell us what you are running or planning and we will suggest the right starting point, with a clear scope agreed before any work begins.

Have an idea worth building?

Tell us what you are trying to build, and we will suggest the right place to start.
